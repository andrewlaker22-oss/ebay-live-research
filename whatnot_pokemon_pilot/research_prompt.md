# Research pilot: Whatnot, U.S. livestream shopping, and Pokémon cards (2019–2022)

You are running a small, source-driven research pilot. Accuracy beats coverage. If you cannot find or verify something, say so plainly; do not fill the gap with a plausible guess.

## Why this research exists

The research is for Andy, who works on eBay Live (eBay's livestream shopping product). Strategy feedback at eBay says three things:
1. People may not go looking for livestream shopping; eBay may need to deliver live content into feeds people already use.
2. Only a small share of people in eBay's target markets shop live at scale, so the real opportunity is growing the whole category, not just winning existing live shoppers.
3. eBay Live currently measures success as viewers who watch 60 seconds or more.

The question behind this pilot: how did Whatnot turn people into livestream shoppers, and did it capture demand that already existed or create new demand? Pokémon cards are the case study because Pokémon collecting existed long before Whatnot.

## Scope (locked)

- United States only.
- Mainly 2019 to 2022, with dated context from later years where it helps.

- Pokémon cards as the only collecting-community case study. Do not build a comparison with other categories. However, if a source says another category (for example sports cards) was larger or grew faster at Whatnot, record that fact, because it bears directly on whether Pokémon drove Whatnot's growth.

- Public sources only. No paywalled databases (no Sensor Tower or Similarweb subscriptions). Free public blog posts and press releases from those companies are fine.
- Aim for 8 to 12 substantive sources. Do not expand beyond that because more material exists.

## Research questions

A. Why did U.S. consumers begin adopting livestream shopping, especially 2019 to 2022? Look at: measured adoption of livestream shopping; the pandemic-era collectibles market; Pokémon card collecting; livestream auctions and pack openings on Instagram Live, YouTube and Twitch before Whatnot. Do not assume these caused one another. Say which links are supported by evidence.

B. Why did Whatnot capture a growing share of U.S. livestream-shopping app downloads? Start from Sensor Tower's June 2022 blog post (https://sensortower.com/blog/live-stream-shopping-2022). Then look at what was happening at Whatnot in each period: when livestream auctions launched, when Pokémon cards launched or grew, seller recruitment, marketing and advertising spend, and features that Instagram Live and ordinary marketplaces lacked (bidding, payment, shipping). TechCrunch reported on Whatnot in December 2020, March 2021 and May 2021.

C. The Pokémon case study.

- What did those platforms lack for selling?
- How did Pokémon contribute to Whatnot's early development? Name specific sellers, creators or communities only if a source names them.
- Did Whatnot mainly attract existing collectors, or is there evidence it brought new people into Pokémon collecting?
- Was Pokémon actually one of Whatnot's main early growth drivers? Do not assume so because it was an early category.

- Is there independent research (not by Whatnot) on why U.S. consumers shop live?

## Evidence rules

- Prefer original documents (company reports, the Sensor Tower post, dated contemporary reporting, survey methodologies) over articles that repeat them.
- Label every figure: company-reported, independent measurement, estimate, or interpretation.
- Give every number its denominator, population, geography and time period. "35% of downloads among 10 apps" is not "35% of live shoppers".
- App downloads are not active users, buyers or sales. Fundraising and valuation are not evidence of consumer adoption. Gross merchandise value is not revenue.
- Separate the date an event happened from the date an article reported it.
- Two things rising at the same time is not proof that one caused the other. Say "coincided with" unless a source shows the link.
- Quote short passages exactly when a claim depends on wording, and give the URL.
- If a page is paywalled or unreachable, say so and do not reconstruct it from memory.

## The four growth mechanisms (organise evidence around these)

1. Existing demand: people already wanted the products (Pokémon cards, collectibles) and live selling was a new way to buy them.
2. Creator and seller relationships: buyers came for, or stayed because of, particular sellers or creators.
3. Platform discovery, distribution and marketing: recommendations, advertising, app-store growth and promotion brought people in.
4. Convenience of buying inside the stream: bidding, payment and shipping built into the live experience.
