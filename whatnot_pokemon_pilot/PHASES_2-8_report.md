# Phases 2 to 8: from Whatnot's history to what it means for eBay Live

Researched and written by Claude, 5 October 2026. Follows `PHASE1_report.md`. Each phase leads with its answer. Sources were read on the original page unless marked **(secondary)**. Phase 6 reuses the repo's existing community analysis; Phase 7 is a desk version because interviews and eBay analytics are not available here.

**The anchor question:** did Whatnot and TikTok LIVE succeed by discovering a new need, or by finding a better way to sell something communities already did?
**Short answer across all phases:** Whatnot is a capture story (existing collector behaviour, better mechanics, then money). TikTok is an attention story where selling was added on top, and in the U.S. most TikTok Shop sales still happen in videos, not live. Top-down pushes that skipped the existing behaviour (TikTok's 2022 UK live shopping, Facebook Live Shopping) failed.

## Phase 2: The first creators and sellers who moved over

**Answer:** the record does not show famous creators bringing big audiences to Whatnot. It shows existing *businesses* (resellers, card sellers, liquidators, convention vendors) moving their selling over, and then finding buyers Whatnot had already gathered. Whatnot supplied the audience more than sellers did. Confidence: medium; few named early sellers are documented.

Evidence:
- Achickrips (Dakota Peters and sisters) sold sneakers on Instagram from 2017 and joined Whatnot in spring 2021, when "the app was only streaming sports trading card sellers." First stream: about $500 to $600 with fewer than 20 viewers. Her verdict: "the buyer base was just there for the taking." First year just over $1M gross; by June 2025, more than $1M GMV per week. (CNBC Make It, 30 Jun 2025, read via NBC New York syndication)
- The earliest growth came from the founders' own stream: Funko sellers "heard about the sale and wanted to do their own" (Inc.), and Whatnot "on-boarded sellers slowly" (LaFontaine, NEA, Sep 2023). YC notes the founders even listed items they did not own and bought them on eBay when they sold, to make the marketplace look liquid (YC blog).
- Later sellers came from other offline or online businesses: an apparel liquidation warehouse (started on Whatnot in 2023), a Storage Wars personality running a card-break business forecast at $15 to 18M in 2025 sales, a fashion seller reaching $1M per month (Fortune, 16 Jun 2025).
- Whatnot's creator programme is a referral scheme ($25 per sign-up), partnered with reselling influencers (Packman Cards, Storage Auction Pirate, Hairy Tornado) (Net Influencer, Mar 2024). It aims mainly at "up-and-coming" creators, not stars.
- A 2025 buyer guide says audiences discover breakers *through the platform* (TikTok's algorithm, Whatnot's browse) rather than following them across platforms (Grumpy Dad Cards, Oct 2025).
- Pokémon collectors in Jan 2022 describe Whatnot as pulling together what they already did on Facebook Live, box-break groups and eBay (Elite Fourum thread, 17 Jan 2022, community forum).

**Verdict on "did they bring audiences or did Whatnot make new stars?":** mostly new stars made on Whatnot from sellers who already had inventory and know-how, not audiences. The breakers profiled by Sports Illustrated in 2020 (Cards Infinity, LiveCaseBreak, etc.) were not traced to Whatnot in any source found.

**Gap:** a seller-by-seller trace of the 2020 YouTube breakers (where they sell today) would settle it. That is a public, doable check.

## Phase 3: How TikTok LIVE turned attention into income

**Answer:** TikTok made attention pay *first* through gifting, years before selling. Shopping was added top-down, failed once (2022), relaunched in the U.S. in September 2023, and grew mainly through short videos. Live is a minority of U.S. TikTok Shop sales (about 10% in 2024, 14% in 2025, third-party estimate). So TikTok shows live *attention* can be monetised by tips, but live *selling* at scale came from video plus an existing audience, not from live itself.

Dated sequence:
| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2015 to 2016 | Musical.ly's live.ly livestreaming app (TikTok's predecessor) | TechCrunch, 27 May 2016 |
| Dec 2020 | Walmart's "Holiday Shop-Along Spectacular": first shoppable livestream with a major U.S. retailer on TikTok; 10 creators; no results published | Tubefilter, 18 Dec 2020 |
| Jul 2022 | TikTok drops plans to expand live shopping to the U.S. and Europe after its UK pilot had "poor sales" and influencers lost interest despite cash incentives | EMARKETER citing the Financial Times, 6 Jul 2022 |
| Aug 2022 | Meta announces Facebook Live Shopping shuts on 1 Oct 2022: "consumers' viewing behaviors are shifting to short-form video" | TechCrunch, 3 Aug 2022 |
| Jul 2023 | "NPC streaming": creators earn from gifts; one reported $2,000 to $3,000 per stream. Creators get about 50% of gift value | CBS News, 27 Jul 2023 |
| 12 Sep 2023 | TikTok Shop launches in the U.S. after testing since Nov 2022: 200,000+ sellers, 100,000+ affiliate creators | TechCrunch |

**When did attention become money?** Gifting, from the live.ly era onward, scaled by 2022 to 2023. **When did selling join?** U.S. launch Sept 2023 after a failed 2022 attempt. **Link to eBay:** Carly's "deliver live into feeds" point fits TikTok: shoppers buy where they already scroll, and the scroll is mostly video. The repo's camera pilot found the same thing: 21 of 50 Shop-tagged camera posts held about 70.9% of views and 0 of 49 captions mentioned live.

## Phase 4: From niche hobby to broad shopping habit

**Answer:** Whatnot entered new categories by importing existing seller communities, and then got cross-category buying from its own buyers. Each new category brought supply from an existing community (vintage vendors, sneaker resellers, liquidators); demand then came partly from Whatnot's existing buyers crossing over (+75% cross-category buying in 2025, company-reported). There is no evidence of Whatnot creating a live habit in a category with no prior community.

Evidence:
- Collectibles stayed the core: sports cards, then Pokémon, then Funko led in 2021. In Apr 2023 collectibles were still the largest category and fashion the fastest-growing; sneakers were showing early wins (Shoe Game Co: $250K in three months) (Modern Retail, 18 Apr 2023).
- Women's fashion GMV grew 5x in 2023; sneakers became the largest fashion subcategory (Modern Retail, 16 May 2024).
- Entry method: in May 2024 Whatnot partnered with ThriftCon to bring 3,000+ existing vintage vendors online "for the first time in ThriftCon's history" (Modern Retail). Celebrities (Lil Yachty, Questlove) were used for sneakers and vinyl visibility (Modern Retail 2023).
- Growth of buyers: GMV over $2B in livestream sales by Oct 2024 (TechCrunch, 2 Oct 2024); about $3B in 2024 (Fortune, Jun 2025); $8B from live sales in 2025 (Whatnot 2026 report). In 2025: 35+ new categories, new buyers +285%, cross-category buying +75%, marketing 10x (CEO letter, Jan 2026).
- Whatnot's three biggest days for new users were 18 to 20 Jan 2025, during the TikTok ban: a spillover of TikTok's live audience, which Whatnot says it did nothing to target (Glossy).
- Why live fits some categories: it "solves category-specific problems": real-time authentication, condition checks, fit and styling (Marketplace Pulse, 26 Jun 2025).

**Parallel to eBay Live entering cameras and luxury:** Whatnot never entered a category without first bringing in a seller community that already sold it. For cameras, the existing community buys through video and listings, not live; an eBay Live camera seller exists in the UK (GBR13), so supply exists in places, but demand-side live behaviour is unproven.

## Phase 5: The downside story

**Answer:** the same mechanics that made Whatnot work (fast auctions, one-swipe bidding, round-the-clock shows, randomised breaks) are now the centre of legal and press criticism. eBay should copy the integrated buying, not the speed and randomness.

Evidence:
- WSJ (Aug 2026, paywalled, read via a secondary relay and The Journal podcast, 14 Aug 2026): one buyer spent $1.3M in four months; criticised features include auctions lasting "three seconds to a minute," single-swipe bids with no confirmation, instant charging, and a 24-hour market. LaFontaine says internal data does not show addiction as a significant problem; Whatnot added optional spending limits, watch-time caps and planned velocity-of-spend alerts. **(secondary)**
- Mar 2026: 30 clients in 15 California arbitration complaints call Whatnot "an unregulated online casino," targeting randomised breaks and repacks; Whatnot: "Gambling isn't allowed on Whatnot" (Baseball America, 16 Mar 2026).
- Jul 2025: a California whistleblower suit calls breaks on Fanatics Live and Whatnot "unlicensed box-break lotteries"; plaintiffs' spending ranged up to over $4M; the state declined to intervene (Gambling Insider).
- Collectors flagged it early: "payments... pulled instantly upon winning a bid... you can lose track of how much money you are spending" (Elite Fourum, Jan 2022). Fortune (Jun 2025) called the design "fertile ground for regrettable impulse buys, or worse, addiction."
- The repo's community data shows the same frictions named by Whatnot buyers and eBay Live buyers alike: sudden-death auctions of 3 to 5 seconds, swipe-bid jumps, notification pressure (OVERVIEW_full_collection.md).

**What not to copy:** randomised break mechanics without clear rules, sub-10-second auctions, no-confirmation bidding, and notification pressure. **What eBay can claim:** authentication and buyer protection are the opposite of the "casino" framing.

## Phase 6: What the behaviour looks like today (testing history against the repo's community data)

**Answer:** the history holds up. Today's live buying still clusters where a pre-existing ritual exists (card breaks, Pokémon openings), runs on Whatnot, Fanatics Live and TikTok Shop, and eBay Live shows up in social data mostly as paid promotion, not as buyer behaviour.

| History says | Today's evidence (repo: `analysis_outputs/full_collection_analysis/`, 8,339 rows, 1,200 read; plus the collector pilot) | Holds? |
|---|---|---|
| Live grows where a ritual already exists | Cards: the ritual is ripping, pulling, grading and card-show negotiation. Break selling is visible on Whatnot, Fanatics Live and TikTok Shop | Yes |
| Platforms win by capturing existing creators/sellers | Collector pilot: Fanatics Live pays hobby creators (#fanaticslivepartner); eBay Live absent from that partner layer | Yes, with eBay behind |
| Mechanics were the edge in 2020 | Whatnot and eBay Live buyers now name the same frictions (fast auctions, notifications). Mechanics are table stakes, not a differentiator | Changed |
| TikTok sells through video, not live | Cameras: Shop-tagged posts held about 70.9% of views; 0 of 49 captions mention live | Yes |
| Top-down pushes look like promotion, not demand | 22 TikTok captions name eBay Live; 13 are paid partnerships; 0 hobby-pull captions name it; 3 first-person purchase accounts in 8,339 rows | Yes |
| Trust is the differentiator | Authentication is the shared trust topic in cards, sneakers, watches, handbags; praised and criticised in the same threads | Yes, contested |

Limits: keyword-selected pulls, not a market sample; views are attention, not purchases; coins are an evidence gap (18 rows).

## Phase 7: People who don't shop live (desk version)

**Answer:** for most U.S. adults the barrier is lack of interest and trust, not lack of discovery. Awareness has risen but the "not interested" share is stable at around four in ten. That supports Carly's "grow the category" goal only if eBay changes *why* people would watch, not just *whether* they see it.

| Date | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Jun 2022 | 17% of U.S. adults ever bought via livestream/video; 6% regularly (n=1,078) | EMARKETER / Bizrate, Oct 2022 |
| 2022 | 78% never participated in a livestream shopping event; 54% uncomfortable shopping through social platforms; 35% worried about quality, seller integrity, shipping | Morning Consult via Retail Dive, 20 Dec 2022 |
| 2022 | 52% of U.S. adults didn't know what livestream shopping was or weren't interested | EMARKETER citing Klarna, Jul 2022 |
| May 2024 | Main reasons for not buying: prefer official sites/stores; scepticism about promoted products; no interest in the products (percentages paywalled) | Rakuten Insight via Statista |
| Aug 2024 | 43.0% have not used and are not interested | EMARKETER / Bizrate, Jan 2025 |
| 2025 | 21.7% of U.S. digital buyers bought via livestream; U.S. livestream e-commerce about $14.6B; convenience (32%) the top barrier per McKinsey | EMARKETER FAQ 2026 |
| Jul 2024 | Whatnot's own buyers watch mainly "to get a product I want" (66%) and for deals (53%) before entertainment (46%) | Whatnot 2024 report |

**On the 60-second measure:** no public source tests whether 60+ seconds of watching predicts buying on any platform. Whatnot's buyers say they come for a specific product, which suggests the predictor is *having the wanted item on stream*, not watch time. This needs eBay's own data: compare purchase rates for 60s+ viewers against viewers who arrived from a search or saved item.

**Interview plan (not done; needs people):** three groups of about 8 each: (1) live buyers, (2) watched but never bought, (3) never tried. Core questions: what were you trying to buy when you first watched; what stopped the first bid; what would you need to see before bidding (comps, authentication, condition proof); where do you already watch hobby content.

## Phase 8: What it means for eBay Live

**Answer:** eBay should capture existing live behaviour where it exists (cards, Pokémon) through the creators and sellers who already run it, and treat categories without live behaviour (cameras, much of luxury) as video-first and authentication-first, not as places a live habit can be created by promotion. The history's one clear warning: no one has created a live shopping habit top-down in the West (TikTok 2022, Facebook 2022), while Whatnot's top-down spend worked only on top of a working bottom-up base.

### Per community: the three questions

| Community | Q1: Is live buying already happening, where? | Q2: Who drives it? | Q3: Opening for eBay Live? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sports cards | Yes: Whatnot (largest category since 2021), Fanatics Live, TikTok Shop breaks | Breakers and card sellers; Fanatics pays hobby creators | Capture: partner with breakers; lead with authentication and transparent break rules (Phase 5) |
| Pokémon / TCG | Yes: Whatnot's #2 category in 2021; openings dominated by prerecorded series | Sellers, opening creators | Capture: seller-run "sealed vs ripped" shows; compete on authenticity and fair comps |
| Sneakers / streetwear | Yes on Whatnot (largest fashion subcategory; fashion fastest-growing) | Resellers who moved from Instagram (Achickrips) | Contested: eBay's edge is authentication; client inputs conflict on priority |
| Luxury (bags, watches) | Some (promoted "mega live" auctions; Vookum on eBay Live) | Dealers and trusted-seller curators | Partial: authenticator-led shows; trust is the product |
| Cameras / electronics | Mostly video, not live (TikTok Shop video; 0/49 captions mention live) | Video creators, Japanese sellers | Create, not capture: test small; video and listings first |
| Coins | Evidence gap | Unknown | Discovery question before investment |

### Hypotheses tied to Carly's points
1. **Reach people in feeds (supported by history):** TikTok's and Meta's pivots show shoppers buy where they already scroll, and that is mostly short video. Clip live moments into video feeds and route back to the stream.
2. **Grow the category (partly supported):** Whatnot's growth rode existing hobby booms plus imports of seller communities. eBay can grow the category mostly by importing communities that already sell (breakers, vintage vendors), not by converting non-shoppers, whose barrier is interest and trust.
3. **60-second measure (unproven):** test against purchase. Whatnot's buyers come for a product; the better leading indicator may be "watched a stream that had an item from my search or watchlist."
4. **Top-down vs bottom-up:** top-down works as an amplifier on a working base (Whatnot 2022, 2025), not as a starter (TikTok UK 2022, Facebook 2022).
5. **What not to copy:** speed and randomness (Phase 5). eBay's distinct position is trust: authentication, buyer protection, comps on screen.

### What would change these conclusions
- eBay Live GMV, repeat rate and conversion by category, and by traffic source (notification, homepage, search).
- The 60s-viewer vs buyer analysis.
- Interviews (Phase 7).
- A trace of where 2020's breakers sell today (Phase 2 gap).
