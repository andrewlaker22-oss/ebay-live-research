# eBay Live community research: overview of the full-collection analysis

Date: 2026-09-28. Provisional and checker-pending. This is the short companion to `REPORT_full_collection.md`; every number and quote there is traceable to a row ID in `claim_ledger.csv`. Read share: 1,200 of 8,339 unique rows read (about one in seven); the rest counted and searched by code; no video watched; no verified transcript exists. Brand posts excluded. Every cited row was checked twice: its ID resolves to one source row, and a key phrase from the part of the row that was actually read supports the claim it is cited for (all 303 cited rows pass; the ledger records distinct creators per claim and shared Reddit rows, counted once). Proportions describe these keyword-selected pulls, not a market.

## What was observed (source text, read in this run)

1. eBay Live is visible in this collection as promotion and seller announcements, not as buyer experience. Every one of the 51 rows naming eBay Live was read: 22 are TikTok captions in the eBay-term pull (13 paid-partnership posts, 9 sellers or hosts announcing shows), 0 are in the hobby-term pull, 13 are YouTube titles (3 of them eBay's own channel), 16 are Reddit and YouTube comments. Four people describe buying on eBay Live in the first person, and all four describe friction: ten-second flash auctions with host quotes the buyer judged 150 to 200 percent above recent eBay solds; a sealed box ripped mid-auction after the bid was placed; a pre-loaded listing that could not be bought outside a show, a queue of 50 to 100 items and 15-second auctions; trade-in promises made on stream and not honoured. No unpaid positive first-person buyer account exists in these pulls. One seller says a comics business "sells a lot on eBay live streams and we do well… our prices are always fmv."
2. The same frictions are described by Whatnot buyers about Whatnot: "Loads of seller do 5s or less", swipe bids that jump, notifications, buying on hype. Live-format friction is not specific to eBay.
3. Authentication is the shared trust topic in every community except coins, and it cuts both ways. Buyers describe AG catching creases a listing hid, refusing a fake Magikarp, or being the reason they buy watches from Japan on eBay; the same threads carry damaged AG-passed cards, an authenticator's mistake costing a $750 sale, and "seller protection is basically a myth". Handbag vetting is community-run; bullion fraud has no AG at all.
4. Each community's own ritual is prerecorded and lives on the hobby side of TikTok and YouTube, where eBay is almost absent from captions: 1 of 62 sports-card, 0 of 87 Pokémon, 0 of 88 sneaker, 1 of 61 luxury, 1 of 188 electronics and 1 of 137 toy hobby-pull captions name eBay. Where eBay is named it is a find venue (vintage clothing, handbags, cameras, dolls, patches, used model trains), a comp source (cards), or a dispute venue (Reddit).
5. Break selling, the live-native card format, appears here as one breaker promoting into TikTok Shop and as four YouTube channels settling breaks on eBay with 26 to 1,317 views per stream, while rip entertainment from two channels on the same lane draws 19,754 to 542,529. Whether break buyers watch on YouTube or on eBay is never stated.
6. Coins: numismatic voice is nearly absent (18 rows match a coin pattern; one graded-coin buyer complaint; the rest bullion fraud). The client's coin green shoot cannot be assessed from this collection.
7. P&A is eBay-native in the eBay pull (engines, clusters, classic-car parts, an Italian scrapyard) with freight, fitment and return friction; nothing links it to live.
8. Absent from these pulls: UK Panini stickers and Premier League (0 rows), match-worn memorabilia (0), Fanatics Live (0), Riftbound (0), German-stated sneaker or card buyers (0). Absence here is a pull fact.

## What is hypothesised (not observed; each has a test in the report)

- Formats that fix the named buyer frictions: sold-comp on screen (coins, cards), no mid-auction item change, on-stream promise enforcement, notification opt-out.
- Seller-hosted live segments that match an existing ritual: AG handling and packaging on camera (cards); legit-check and AG walkthrough with an authenticator (sneakers; risk of reading as another #ad); "how I vet a listing / my trusted sellers" and Japanese-seller preloved segments (handbags); "what AG checks on a watch" (watches); "does it work" tests (cameras, used trains); "the collection goes up for sale live" (toys).
- Route in every case: the seller's or creator's own handle first, clipped to @eBay or @eBayLive; a dedicated handle only if a series proves itself. Nothing in the community evidence argues for a dedicated handle now.

## What the community evidence says to each business-side input

The framework is Community Opportunity × Business Opportunity × Right to Play. This collection covers only the community side; it cannot rank communities.
- Live GMV and buyer dynamics by category: unknown here. If cards and TCG carry Live, the buyers who talk are comp- and condition-checkers, and the visible frictions are speed, mid-auction changes and AG handling.
- Supply: sellers in these rows judge eBay Live by fees, viewers, "clientele" and its effect on their store traffic; several r/Ebay sellers call it force-fed. Seller opinion, not buyer data.
- Competitive position: Whatnot is described by its own sellers as a liquidation venue and by its buyers with the same complaints made about eBay Live; TikTok Shop is where the hobby pull's live promotion goes (breaks, clone consoles, preloved bags, patches).
- Right to play: authentication is eBay's visible differentiator in cards, sneakers, watches and luxury, and its reputation is contested in each. AG logistics (hub delays) are a named friction.
- Sneakers: the call deprioritised them for Live; the sprint Q&A lists them as right-to-win. The heat is real; eBay's role there is authentication. Reported, not resolved.
- Coins and P&A: no community evidence either way for Live; discovery questions.

## Missing business inputs

Live GMV, buyer counts and repeat rates by category; seller supply per category; the share of break buyers watching on eBay versus YouTube; return rates on live-sold items versus listings; notification opt-out rates; any native eBay Live chat or transaction data. None of these are in the eight files.
