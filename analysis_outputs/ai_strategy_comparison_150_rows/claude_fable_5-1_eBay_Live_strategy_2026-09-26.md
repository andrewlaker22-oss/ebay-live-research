# eBay Live: provisional strategy from the 150-row sample

Model: Claude Fable 5.1. Input: TEST_150_rows_eBay_Live_AI_comparison_sample.xlsx. Citations use tab name plus the first 8 characters of flash_evidence_row_id (reddit/youtube IDs in full).

**1. Thesis.** Focus eBay Live on one behavioral group: comp-driven card buyers. Sports-card and Pokémon collectors who buy singles against recent sold prices, use grading and Authenticity Guarantee (AG), and treat auction endings as a skill. Not "cards" as a category, not the break-and-rip gambler. Sports and Pokémon behave as one hobby here: 4 of 15 card-labelled TikToks carry both labels, and both subreddits argue about the same PSA and eBay mechanics. Live fits because auction endings, comps and grading are already real-time, chat-driven behaviors. Promising but unproven: no row shows a buyer choosing eBay Live over Whatnot or TikTok Live.

**2a. eBay's card mechanics are already what this community argues about.** Of 25 direct-eBay TikToks, the two most-commented are card videos about eBay mechanics: a $5,000 scam story (622 comments; TikTok Videos, local_f19c) and Extended Bidding ending sniping (230 comments, 464 shares; local_269d). A third teaches winning "cards under comps" with a sniping tool (local_41e9). Reddit buyers already route $1,000 raw cards through AG and complain about packaging, not the guarantee (r/baseballcards, reddit:t3_1todhm0, score 453, all 4 comments). Viewers of the Pokémon-comps YouTube video say comping is hard and tax plus shipping negate deals (youtube_comment:UgyAckhLXaLwteWIaTp4AaABAg, Ugzb2qsNhRmTAKWW_h54AaABAg). Comments are not purchase intent, but the vocabulary is eBay-native.

**2b. The existing live ritual is the break, competitors own it, and buyers are skeptical of it.** Card videos are 15 of 50 TikToks, and the break format sells through TikTok Shop ("daily breaks located in our Tiktok Shop", local_6382); a pricing tool markets to "whatnot, TikTok and eBay live stream" (local_4476). The top comment under the sports-card mystery-pack video (283,653 views) is "I'd never buy them" (174 likes; youtube_comment:UgwqtQX5A6G63Tr_fzB4AaABAg). Only 3 of 50 TikToks mention a livestream, none inviting buyers to eBay Live. eBay Live should not copy breaks; it should own the singles auction with the comp on screen.

**3. Test: "Comp Check Live."** Weekly 60-minute eBay Live singles auction hosted by an established card seller-creator already teaching comps and sniping on TikTok. Each lot shows its last three sold prices and grade, AG eligibility, an opening bid below comp, and the host explains Extended Bidding on air. Route: the seller's own TikTok posts 20-second "lots versus comps" clips (the local_41e9 format) linking to the stream; eBay's existing handle cross-posts. No new dedicated handle. Support: unique and repeat bidders per lot rise across four streams, with lots clearing near comp. Reject: viewers arrive but lots go unsold or clear far below comp, meaning a bargain hunt, not a trusted venue.

**4. Reality check.** Counterexample: the sample's only buyer-facing live invite is preloved luxury handbags ("Come to my live stream check the condition", local_0b94), and the handbag seller thread (reddit:t3_1o1wuqt) shows condition disputes are that subcommunity's core pain, which live inspection solves. Handbags may be the better live fit; cards may only be the better eBay fit. Missing fact most likely to change this: native eBay Live chat and sell-through by category, since every live inference comes from non-live sources.
